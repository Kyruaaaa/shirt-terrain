Shirt Terrain

The T-Shirt is a site where grass and geography are born and sprawl, forming landscapes and maps. Cleaning is renewing and erasing.

Site: a T-shirtOct 2026

Observe

Where are the edges of the site? What's inside and what's outside it?
The edges are the ribbed collar band, the folded sleeve hems and the bottom hem, where fabric ends and skin or air begins. Where it is no longer felt but seen.
Who uses it? When, and for how long? Is their attention a glance, a wait, or a scroll?
I uses it for hours and barely looks at it. Everyone else sees it in a passing glance of about a second, long enough to read one word: LUCKY. The longest viewers are things that touch it: my skin, a rock, a washing machine.
What happens there now? How does it change across a day or a week?
In a day it picks up dust, air and seeds, and stretches at the shoulders. Over a week it runs a cycle: worn, marked, washed, dried, folded ; Repeat. Each wash erases most marks but pulls more threads loose, freeing.
What are its formats and specs?
A fitted white tee in a fine vertical rib knit, with a crew neck and short sleeves. On the chest is a hand-stitched appliqué: a duck cut from yellow gingham with a tan wing patch, a fabric beak and a yellow button eye, under the word LUCKY cut from green gingham. Every piece is tacked down with pale green cross-stitches and the edges are left raw.
Who controls it, what are the rules, and who would you ask for permission?
I control it: what it says, where it goes, when it gets washed. Rules fluctuates with my preception, my tender towards it are the only permission stages.
What details repeat across the site? What's an anomaly?
The vertical ribs repeat across the whole shirt. The gingham grid repeats inside the duck and every letter, and the green cross-stitch repeats around every patch edge. The anomaly born from how the sun rises and falls.
What's present, and what's missing?
Present: body heat, smell, creases, a handmade image with a word, and raw edges already shedding threads. Missing: any record of where the shirt has been. The duck stays the same while everything around it gets marked and washed away.

Interpret

What's unique about this site?
It is a landscape that is born and erased every week. It moves through the world on a body and records its contact with everything it touches, and it is private (against skin) and public (seen by strangers) at once.
What can you do only at this site?
I can be sentient about the prescence of it or ignorant about the abscence of it.
What does the site expect from the people who use it? What would surprise them?
It expects to be worn, washed and ignored, and it expects its image, the lucky duck, to stay the same. A shirt that grows its own terrain around the duck and loses it again would surprise people.
What does this site share with other sites you know?
With a tattoo, it is on the body and seen in fragments. With a billboard, it is seen in a glance by people passing. With the granite boulders at Lincoln Woods, it carries a record of weathering by wind and water, only on a clock of days instead of ten thousand years.

Respond

How does this site relate to your work?
I am drawn to nature and to slow processes like growth and weathering. The shirt lets me treat the body as a weather system and the fabric as ground that weathers in hours.
What excites you about it? What bores you?
The raw edges of the appliqué excite me, because they already fray and weather with every wash, like an eroding coastline. The require of myself to catalyse transformation bores me.
Describe a hypothetical work for this site in one sentence.
Visualize the geography, geology, geomorphology that make this t-shirt in prescence and conversation with me.
Name 3 ways that work could take shape there.
  1. Geography: a garden in conversation. A 3D twin of the shirt where each touch stitches a small wildflower into the cloth and each wash fades it, so the landscape only exists while I am in conversation with it.
  2. Geology: strata of wearing. Photograph the shirt after every wear and wash, and stack the images as layers of sediment, so a month of days reads like rock.
  3. Geomorphology: the shape of creases. Trace how the shirt folds, stretches and creases on my body, and draw those folds as a contour map of a terrain that rises and erodes each week.
